Entering the Philippines with no local entity

I chose the Employer of Record, negotiated its price down 61%, and hired the first team into a market where the company had no presence.

Market:Philippines, no local entity
Scope:Provider selection, negotiation, first-team hiring

Situation

The business wanted a physical team in the Philippines to show a local presence when pitching to clients, and hiring there cost less than in other markets. With no legal entity in the country, an Employer of Record (EOR) was the only route.

My role

I owned the whole process: provider selection, commercial negotiation, and hiring the first team members myself.

Who I worked with

I convinced the APAC Managing Director to approve the EOR route. I worked with the Legal team on the contract and compliance review, and aligned with Finance on invoices and deposits. I worked with the new employees to complete their contracts and documents.

How I chose the provider

I set four criteria: price, quality of advice, speed of response, and a smooth experience for employees. I interviewed a number of providers across several rounds, setting our requirements against what each offered. The smaller, location-based providers were equally expensive or more expensive. I chose a provider with a presence in many countries, because if we expanded into another market without an entity, we could use the same provider again.

The negotiation

I used competing offers as leverage and cut the per-employee monthly price by 61% on the same billing basis. I have kept the rate unchanged through every renewal.

Hiring the first team

Candidates were being asked to join a company with no local entity, no office and no local brand, employed through a third party many had never heard of. I gave each candidate everything I knew, explained the structure with a visa analogy (a specialist agency handles the paperwork, but the visa stays in your name), and made clear that the EOR is the legal employer while our company directs the work. When I did not know an answer, I said so, found it and came back. None of the candidates declined because of the structure.

Outcome

Ten employees moved onto the EOR model. The core team signed. The Philippines team has since grown across several teams, and some of our strongest talent is now based there.

What I would change

Two of the criteria I set were speed of response and a smooth employee experience. Those are the areas where the provider has been weakest.

Support tickets are handled by whoever is available, so context has to be repeated, and our point of contact has changed several times. If I ran the selection again, I would weigh relationship continuity as heavily as global reach. We have identified a provider with a dedicated contact and plan to migrate next year.
